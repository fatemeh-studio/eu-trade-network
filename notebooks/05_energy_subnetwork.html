05 · Energy subnetwork (HS-27)

RQ4 — is energy trade a structurally different market? Same 35 economies, only HS chapter 27 (coal, crude, refined products, gas, electricity). Compare concentration, who leads, and where Austria sits against the all-merchandise network.

Why the node set is held fixed, and the caveats that matter for energy (2022 prices, suppliers outside the frame, pipeline-gas misattribution), are on the Data & method page.

Bridge. HS-2716 electrical energy is the customs view of the same physical flow that austria-energy-analysis measures hourly from ENTSO-E.

The figure (RQ4 headline)

Left: how fast cumulative export share piles up as you walk down the exporter ranking — the steeper curve is the more concentrated market. Right: each economy’s share of energy exports against its share of merchandise exports. The dotted diagonal is “energy share = merchandise share”, so everything above it is an economy that punches above its trade weight in energy.

Austria’s energy partners

Who Austria buys HS-27 from and who it sells to, as a share of its own energy imports / exports inside this node set.

Show code 
def _partner_shares(edges: pd.DataFrame, side: str, partner: str) -> pd.Series:
    """Partner shares of Austria's energy trade on one side of the edge."""
    own = edges.loc[edges[side] == FOCUS]
    totals = own.groupby(partner)["value_kusd"].sum().sort_values(ascending=False)
    return totals / totals.sum()


suppliers = _partner_shares(energy_edges, "importer_iso3", "exporter_iso3")
customers = _partner_shares(energy_edges, "exporter_iso3", "importer_iso3")

top_partners = pd.DataFrame(

def _share(mix_frame: pd.DataFrame, hs: str) -> float:
    """Share of one HS heading in a composition table (0.0 if absent)."""
    row = mix_frame.loc[mix_frame["hs"] == hs, "share"]
    return float(row.iloc[0]) if len(row) else 0.0


def _value(mix_frame: pd.DataFrame, hs: str) -> float:

A different market. Energy is 12% of the value in this network but a structurally different market. The top 3 exporters carry 55% of energy exports (RUS, NOR, USA) against 38% for merchandise as a whole (CHN, DEU, USA); the Herfindahl index is 0.124 vs 0.075, i.e. 8 effective suppliers instead of 13. The leaders change too: the manufacturing hubs that dominate merchandise trade cannot sell resources they do not have, so resource exporters take their place — Russia and Norway sit far above the diagonal in the right-hand panel, Germany and China well below it. Concentration, not connectivity, is again the risk — the energy graph is nearly complete (density 0.96), so everyone trades with everyone; the volume just sits on very few sources.

Caveat on the year. 2022 is the energy-crisis year: BACI values are USD, so the energy subnetwork’s 12% value share reflects prices as much as volumes, and Russia still leads the 2022 table despite sanctions that were only partly in force. Read the concentration ranking as a snapshot of a market under stress, not a structural constant.

Austria. Austria is 12th of 35 by energy exports (0.9% of the network’s energy) and 18th by energy imports — versus 17th and 15th for merchandise. It is a net energy importer (-5.5 bn USD). The ranking flatters it: Austria produces almost no fossil fuel, so its energy exports are re-exports and transit — 53% electricity, 26% gas and 17% refined products passed on to neighbours. Its imports are equally intermediated: 59% arrive from DEU, which is a routing fact, not a production one.

The frame misses the real suppliers. Only 48% of this node set’s energy imports come from inside it, against 65% for merchandise; for Austria it is 81% vs 90%. Europe’s crude and LNG largely originate outside the 35 economies modelled here (the Gulf, Kazakhstan, North and West Africa). The energy subgraph therefore describes how energy is redistributed within Europe, not where it ultimately comes from — a full dependence analysis needs the world node set.

Pipeline gas is badly captured — do not read dependence off it. Austria’s recorded HS-2711 (gas) imports inside this node set are only 0.4 bn USD while its gas exports are 2.9 bn, which would make Austria a net gas exporter in 2022. It was not: it burned mostly Russian pipeline gas. Customs statistics attribute a flow to the declared consignment partner, so gas crossing hubs and traded contracts lands on the wrong edge. Treat merchandise data as evidence about value routed, and use physical flow data for dependence.

Bridge to austria-energy-analysis. Electricity (HS-2716) is 9% of the energy network’s value but 53% of Austria’s energy exports and 35% of its energy imports — 5.9 bn USD out against 5.7 bn in, an almost perfectly balanced position with its neighbours (DEU, CHE, HUN, SVN). That is the same physical flow the sibling project measures hourly from ENTSO-E: there, Austria’s renewable share of electricity reaches 86% (2024), gas generation falls by a third, and each GW of wind+solar cuts the day-ahead price by 14.3 EUR/MWh. Read together: this project locates Austria in Europe’s energy exchange (mid-sized, intermediating, import-dependent), the other one explains what the exchange is for.
